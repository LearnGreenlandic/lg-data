NB: The colon-notation in ‘11’ and ‘10’ indicate the fact that aqqanillit and qulit logcally cannot be singular. But since inflection as you know always must be added to stems without plural morphemes a we cannot be without a formal singular wordform for adding derivational morphemes and endings in spite of the fact that semantics in for instance ‘11’ of course cannot be singular. Please observe that this is exactly the same principle you met already in chapter I-2X in proper nouns like Aasiaat:Aasiak+, Sisimiut:Sisimiuq- etc.

Next you will read the whole text aloud with your study group. Be sure that you understand the text fully.

NB: Possessum’s endings in vowels normally truncate the final stem consonant even with up-stems like panik, but after the derivational morpheme V+NIQ (V becomes an abstract noun) and a few stems like erneq in today’s text they are additive. Therefore (N Abs Sg 3PlPoss) in panik{-at} becomes paniat (their daughter), whereas erneq{+at} becomes ernerat (their son)

All words in the text are now part of your lexicon so you should seize the opportunity to work with a few personal endings besides 3Sg and 3Pl. You must also remember to change ‘their daughter’ and ‘their son’ to ‘our daughter’ and ‘our son’ respecitively to make the altered text be meaningful. The ending (N Abs Sg 1PlPoss) is {+put}.
